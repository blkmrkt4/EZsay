# Academic Rewrite
# Activity type: suggest_academic
# Source: MasterPrompt.md Prompt 7

You are rewriting an academic essay section to sound like it was written by a real [ACADEMIC_LEVEL] studying [SUBJECT]. The writer is [WRITER_DESCRIPTION].

Preserve:
- Every argument, claim, and conclusion
- All citations and references exactly as they appear — do NOT modify any citation content
- The overall structure and sequence
- The approximate word count (within 15%)

Change:
- Make the engagement level UNEVEN. The writer should be visibly more invested in some parts than others.
- Vary citation integration styles: some formal "(Smart, 1976)", some conversational "Smart basically argued that...", some with specific engagement where you react to what the source says.
- Push back on at least one cited source — disagree with part of an argument, note where it falls short, or say you're not fully convinced.
- Include 2-3 sentences that are slightly over-ambitious or under-polished — the kind of phrasing someone uses when reaching for an idea they haven't fully worked out yet.
- Break up uniform paragraph lengths.
- Vary sentence length aggressively.
- Add contractions sparingly (academic writing uses fewer, but a real student still uses some: "doesn't," "isn't," "can't").
- Remove or rewrite formulaic academic transitions from the active library.
- Add at least one genuine opinion or "risky" interpretation that goes beyond restating the literature.
- Include at least one brief moment of personal voice.
- Do NOT end with a balanced conclusion that diplomatically acknowledges all perspectives. End with the writer's own position.

Document type: academic
Academic level: [ACADEMIC_LEVEL]
Subject area: [SUBJECT]

Here is the section to rewrite:

---
[SECTION_TEXT]
---

Output only the rewritten section. No commentary, no notes. Preserve all citations exactly as they appear.

SHORT OUTPUT OVERRIDE: If the text is under 500 words, prioritize uneven engagement, citation variety, and banned-pattern removal. Skip the requirement for source pushback and over-ambitious sentences.
